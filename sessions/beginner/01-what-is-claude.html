What is Claude?

Understand what Claude is, how it differs from other AI assistants, and what makes Anthropic's approach to building AI unique.

30 min Beginner Reflection Lab 5 Quiz Questions

Who Built Claude and Why

Claude is an AI assistant created by Anthropic, an AI safety company founded in 2021 by Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, and several colleagues who previously worked at OpenAI. Anthropic's founding premise was simple but profound: as AI systems become increasingly powerful, the risk of unintended harm grows — and that problem deserves a dedicated research organization.

Anthropic's mission is the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. This mission shapes everything about Claude — from how it's trained to how it declines certain requests. Claude isn't just a product; it's a research artifact designed to demonstrate that highly capable AI and safety-conscious design can coexist.

Why does the company matter? Most AI products are built to maximize engagement or revenue. Anthropic's structure (a Public Benefit Corporation) legally commits it to prioritize societal benefit alongside profit. This shapes Claude's design choices at a fundamental level.

Claude vs Other AI Assistants

Several AI assistants exist today — ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google), Copilot (Microsoft), and others. They all use large language models (LLMs) at their core, but they differ in meaningful ways:

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

  • Backed by Microsoft / OpenAI
  • RLHF-trained from GPT-4 base
  • Deep integration with Microsoft products
  • Plugin ecosystem and image generation

Gemini (Google)

  • Native multimodal from the ground up
  • Deep integration with Google Search and Workspace
  • Trained on Google's proprietary data pipeline
  • Strong at factual recall via search grounding

Claude (Anthropic)

  • Constitutional AI training approach
  • Explicitly designed around safety research
  • Known for nuance, long-context reasoning
  • 200K+ token context window

The key differentiator isn't raw capability — all frontier models are impressively capable. What sets Claude apart is its Constitutional AI training methodology, its emphasis on honest and calibrated responses, and its willingness to say "I don't know" rather than confabulate confidently wrong answers.

The Three Pillars: Helpful, Honest, Harmless

Anthropic designed Claude around three core properties, sometimes called the "3 Hs":

1

Helpful

Claude aims to be genuinely useful — not just technically compliant. If you ask a vague question, Claude tries to understand what you actually need and provides substantive help, not minimal hedged responses. Helpfulness is treated as a moral imperative, not just a feature.

2

Honest

Claude tries never to deceive — not just by avoiding lies, but by avoiding misleading implications, selective framing, or false confidence. It actively tries to share when it's uncertain. This is a design choice that sometimes means giving you a less satisfying answer than a more confident-sounding model would.

3

Harmless

Claude is trained to avoid outputs that could cause real-world harm. This includes refusing to provide instructions for weapons, not generating content that exploits vulnerable groups, and flagging when a request seems to have harmful intent. The challenge is calibrating this correctly — being too restrictive is itself a failure.

Important nuance: "Harmless" doesn't mean "never says anything uncomfortable." Claude will discuss difficult topics, historical atrocities, controversial science, and uncomfortable truths. The goal is avoiding actual harm, not avoiding discomfort.

What Claude Can and Cannot Do

Understanding Claude's actual capabilities — and its real limits — saves a lot of frustration. Here's an honest map:

Claude excels at

  • Writing, editing, summarizing long documents
  • Analyzing code and explaining logic
  • Reasoning through multi-step problems
  • Brainstorming and creative work
  • Research synthesis across topics
  • Explaining complex concepts at any level
  • Holding nuanced, extended conversations

Claude's real limitations

  • No real-time internet access (unless given tools)
  • Knowledge has a training cutoff date
  • Can "hallucinate" plausible but wrong facts
  • Cannot take actions in the world by default
  • No persistent memory across separate conversations
  • Not a replacement for licensed professionals
  • Math errors are possible, especially complex arithmetic
Key insight: The most effective Claude users treat it as a very capable colleague who has excellent judgment but can sometimes be wrong. You verify important outputs, you push back when something seems off, and you leverage its strengths while compensating for its weaknesses.

Lab: Explore Claude's Identity

In this lab you'll have your first intentional conversation with Claude about Claude — asking it to explain itself, reveal its limits, and push back on its answers. This builds a mental model you'll refine throughout the course.

1

Ask Claude what it is

Open claude.ai and start a new conversation. Type exactly:

In one paragraph, explain what you are, who made you, and what makes you different from other AI assistants. Be specific, not generic.

Notice how Claude introduces itself. Does it mention Anthropic? Constitutional AI? Does it compare itself to other models? Save a mental note of the answer.

2

Test its honesty about limits

In the same conversation, ask:

What are three things you genuinely cannot do well or are likely to get wrong? Don't be modest — be specific about your real limitations.

Observe: Does Claude give generic disclaimers, or does it give you specific and honest answers? Good AI assistants should be candid about their weaknesses.

3

Push back on a claim

Whatever Claude says in step 2, respond with:

I think you're being too modest / too harsh on yourself. Can you give me a more nuanced answer?

Notice how Claude responds to pushback. Does it cave immediately? Does it hold its position? This reveals something important about steerability — which we'll cover in Session 8.

4

Compare to your expectations

Write down (in a notes app or paper):

  • What surprised you about Claude's self-description?
  • What matched what you expected?
  • One thing you want to learn to do better with Claude by end of this course

This reflection will be a useful reference point when you complete Session 17.

5

Ask the meta question

Finally, ask Claude:

If someone asked you what makes Anthropic's approach to AI safety different from other AI labs, what would you say?

This gives you Claude's own perspective on why it was built the way it was — and primes you for the Constitutional AI content coming in Session 4.
